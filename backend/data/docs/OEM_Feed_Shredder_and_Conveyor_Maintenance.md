# OEM Manual Extract — Feed Shredder and Conveyor Lines (Shredder 1 / 2 Full Line)

Applies to: feed-hall shredding lines for press mud, napier grass and agri residue — feed hopper, magnetic separator,
stone trap, rotor shredder with counter-knives, and the belt / screw conveyors to the mixing tanks.

## Safety
- Stop and isolate the full line under LOTO (SOP-ROT-01) before opening the cutting chamber, clearing a jam,
  touching a belt or cleaning a pulley. A pull-cord or e-stop is NOT an isolation.
- The rotor can turn under its own weight or stored material pressure after stopping — chock it before working near blades.
- Never clear a jam by hand. Use the tools supplied.
- Ear defenders near the shredder; FFP2 dust mask in dry season; gloves at the magnet (strong pull on tools).

## Blades and counter-knives
- Check blades weekly; take a photo for the PM record.
- Rotate / re-sharpen / replace by tonnage processed (see nameplate; typically every 1,500–3,000 t on agri residue,
  much sooner with sandy feed). Always change as a set.
- Counter-knife gap: set to OEM value (typically 0.5–1.0 mm) after any blade work.
- Rounded edges = normal wear. Nicks and chips = stones or metal; check the magnet and stone trap.
- Target cut length 20–40 mm. Longer fibres wrap pump and agitator impellers and form crust in digesters.

## Foreign-object protection
- Overband magnet must be ON and cleaned every shift. Interlock shredder start with magnet running where possible.
- Empty the stone trap every shift (more often for field-collected feed).
- Inspect loads on the tipping floor; remove twine, plastic, sacks, stones and iron.

## Feeding practice
- Feed at or below rated capacity (t/h on the nameplate) in small, steady loader scoops; keep the hopper half full.
- Blend wet press mud or rain-soaked grass with drier residue. Squeeze test: if water runs out, it is too wet.
- Watch motor current: swings with each dump = overfeeding; high with light feed = blunt blades; spike with a bang = foreign object.

## Drive
- Gearbox oil: grade on the lube label, level 60–80 % of sight glass, change yearly or at 8,000 h.
- Rotor bearings: grease to the chart (quantity and interval); fit labyrinth seals against fibre dust.
- Belt drive: matched sets only; tension by deflection gauge; align pulleys after any change.
- Bearing temperature < 80 °C; vibration < 4.5 mm/s. Stop at > 85 °C.

## Conveyors
- Tracking: belt centred on head and tail pulleys. Re-track with the tail take-up, a little at a time.
- Tension: to OEM value; recheck 24 h after a new belt or joint.
- Scrapers: inspect weekly; adjust blade pressure; replace worn blades. Carry-back of wet press mud builds up on
  pulleys and pushes the belt off track.
- Idlers: replace seized idlers — they cut the belt.
- Skirt rubbers and chute seals: check monthly; replace when worn to stop dust and spillage.
- Zero-speed / rotation sensors: clean and check the gap (typically 3–5 mm) monthly; a dusty sensor gives nuisance trips.

## Dust extraction
- Check the dust collector fan and bags weekly; read the bag-filter dP gauge.
- Light water mist at the hopper in the dry season; keep feed-hall doors closed on windy days.

## Preventive maintenance schedule
| Interval | Task |
|---|---|
| Every shift | Magnet on and clean; stone trap emptied; noise; belt tracking; spillage |
| Daily | Clean pulleys and scrapers; wipe zero-speed sensors in dusty / monsoon conditions |
| Weekly | Blades and counter-knives (photo), cut length, rotor bearing grease, scraper check |
| Monthly | Vibration and IR on drive; belt tension; skirts and seals; dust collector; sensor gap |
| By tonnage | Blade set rotation / replacement; counter-knife gap reset |
| Yearly | Gearbox oil change; bearing overhaul; idler survey; motor megger test before monsoon |

## Typical failure modes
- Blunt blades — throughput falls, current stays high, cut length grows.
- Foreign object — bang, current spike, trip; chipped blades.
- Wet / long feed — wrapping on the rotor, bridging in the hopper, belt slip.
- Overfeeding — current swings, hopper overflow, overload trips.
- Conveyor mistracking / slip — spillage, edge wear, squeal.
- Drive bearing / gearbox wear — growl, heat, vibration.
- Dust escape — worn skirts, weak extraction.
- Nuisance trips — dusty zero-speed sensor or overload set too low.
